How Strong is the Relationship?

Read the keys. Decide whether the relationship is strong (identifying) or weak (non-identifying). Then choose the line the ERD should use.

Question 1 of 8 13%

The line is not about how many.

Cardinality tells you how many instances may participate. Relationship strength tells you whether the parent/determinant key becomes part of the child/dependent primary key.

Quick key

Ask one question: Did the parent/determinant PK become part of the child/dependent PK?

Solid line Strong (Identifying) relationship. The parent/determinant PK is part of the child/dependent PK.
Dashed line Weak (Non-identifying) relationship. The parent/determinant PK migrates only as an FK.
Do not use cardinality to decide strength. A 1:M relationship can be strong (identifying) or weak (non-identifying). Optionality can also be present in either case.

For educators

This activity targets a common ER-modeling confusion: students often treat line style as another form of cardinality or assume existence dependence automatically makes a relationship identifying. The activity instead makes them inspect the primary-key structure first, then connect the key structure to the Crow’s Foot line.

  • Round 1: recognize the pattern from PK/FK structure.
  • Round 2: compare the same business relationship implemented two different ways.
  • Round 3: apply the rule to business records, weak entities, and bridge entities.
  • Immediate feedback explains both the database logic and the business consequence.
  • No account, submission system, external script, or data collection.
